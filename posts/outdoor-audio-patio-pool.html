Outdoor Audio for Patios and Pools in the Bahamas

Outdoor living is a big part of life here, and good sound makes it better. The outdoors is a demanding environment for audio, so plan it properly.

By Island IntegrationNov 2, 20265 min read

Choose speakers built for the outdoors

Indoor speakers do not survive long outside. Look for products designed and rated for outdoor use, with weather-resistant materials, and consider how they will handle sun, rain and salt air.

In the Bahamas the environment is harder than the word "outdoor" usually implies. Afternoon sun bakes anything facing west, summer rain arrives sideways, and a home near the water gets a constant film of salt on every surface. The differences between a speaker that lasts and one that fails in a couple of seasons are in the details: a sealed cabinet, a grille that will not rust, and a driver cone and surround that can take ultraviolet exposure without cracking. Mounting hardware matters too. Plated steel brackets stain a wall with rust streaks within a year near the sea; stainless brackets and fasteners avoid that entirely.

Speaker types

  • Landscape speakers blend into gardens and planting beds for even, low-profile sound. They are usually paired with an in-ground subwoofer, so the small satellite units can be kept at a modest level while the bass fills in underneath. This is the approach that gives the best coverage over a large garden or pool deck.
  • Wall or eave-mounted speakers suit covered patios and lanais. Under a roof they are shielded from the worst of the rain and sun, and they work well for a seating or dining area that does not need to cover a wide space.
  • Pool-deck speakers should be placed where they are protected from direct splash and rated accordingly. Keep them back from the coping and away from where towels are shaken out and chairs are dragged.
  • Rock and bollard speakers sit in planting or along paths and can be used where there is no wall or roof to mount to.

Zone the sound

Separate zones for the patio, pool and garden let you play music where people are and keep it quiet elsewhere. Volume, source and on/off can be controlled from a keypad or app, the way an integrated system handles indoor rooms.

Zoning also solves a practical problem: a covered patio and an open pool deck need very different volume levels to sound the same to the people in them. If both run from a single amplifier channel, one area is always too loud or too quiet. Give each area its own amplifier channel and its own volume control and the problem goes away.

When the outdoor zones are part of a multi-room audio and video system, the same playlist can follow you from the kitchen to the pool, or a dinner party can have one source outside and a different one in the living room. A keypad by the patio door is often more useful than an app, because guests and staff can turn the music on or off without needing anyone’s phone.

Be a considerate neighbor

Sound carries over water and open space. Aiming speakers inward and using several lower-volume speakers instead of a few loud ones keeps coverage even and spill low. Check any local noise rules that apply to your area.

The instinct is to put two big speakers on the house wall and point them out across the pool. That gives you loud sound near the house, thin sound at the far end, and a clear signal to everyone along the canal. The better layout is the reverse: smaller speakers around the edge of the area, facing back toward the seating, each playing at a modest level. People in the space hear full, even sound; people beyond it hear very little. In a gated community or along a shared waterfront, this is the difference between enjoying your system and getting complaints.

A volume limit set in the control system is a good safeguard for a rental: the zone simply cannot be turned up past a level you have decided is reasonable.

Power and wiring

Run cable, power and conduit to outdoor locations during construction or landscaping when it’s easiest. Use cable rated for burial or outdoor exposure, and have the connections sealed properly.

Outdoor cable should go in conduit wherever it is buried, with a spare pull string left in place. Conduit protects against shovels, roots and rodents, and it means a damaged cable can be replaced without digging up the garden. Every splice should be inside a sealed, weatherproof enclosure above ground level, with a drip loop so water runs away from the entry rather than into it.

Amplifiers stay indoors in the equipment rack, not in a cabinet outside, and long runs to a far corner of the property may need heavier-gauge cable to avoid losing output. If you are building new, add the outdoor speaker and conduit runs to your pre-wire checklist so they go in with the rest of the structured cabling.

Hurricane season and off-season care

Between June and November, outdoor equipment should be part of your storm preparation. Landscape speakers and wall units are generally designed to stay in place, but anything on a removable bracket can be taken down and stored before a major storm. After a storm, rinse salt deposits off grilles and housings with fresh water and check that cable entries have not been disturbed.

On a second home that sits empty for months, have the outdoor zones checked as part of a regular service visit.

How we approach it

We start with how you use the space: where people sit, where they swim, whether you entertain large groups and how close your neighbors are. From that we lay out the zones, choose speaker types for each area and plan the cable routes so they go in with the landscaping or construction. The outdoor zones then appear on the same keypads and app as every other room.

Frequently asked questions

Can I just use a portable Bluetooth speaker?

For a small balcony, yes. For a pool and garden, a portable speaker is loud in one spot and inaudible everywhere else, it has to be carried in every evening, and it will not last long in salt air. A permanent system covers the whole area evenly and is always ready.

Will outdoor speakers disturb the house?

Not if they are placed and aimed properly. Speakers facing inward toward the seating area, at moderate volume, put very little sound back toward bedroom windows.

Do outdoor speakers need maintenance?

A fresh-water rinse now and then, a visual check of brackets and cable entries, and a look after any big storm. Properly specified equipment needs little else.
