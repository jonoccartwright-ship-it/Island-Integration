Starlink in the Bahamas: Setting It Up Properly

Starlink has changed what is possible for homes on New Providence and across the Family Islands. The dish is the easy part. The mounting, the network behind it and the plan for storms decide whether it works well.

By Island IntegrationNov 16, 20263 min read

Satellite internet used to be the last resort. Starlink has made it a real option for streaming, video calls and cameras, including on islands where fibre and cable do not reach. We now see it as the main connection in Family Island homes and as a backup connection in Nassau. In both cases the same few things separate a good installation from a frustrating one.

Mounting: sky view and wind

The dish needs a wide, clear view of the sky. Palms, neighbouring roofs and masts cause short dropouts that show up as frozen video calls. Use the obstruction check in the Starlink app before choosing a position, and check it at the height the dish will actually sit.

Then think about wind. A dish on a pole clamped to a fascia board is not a hurricane-season installation. Use a proper mount fixed to structure, seal every roof penetration, and run the cable in conduit where it is exposed to sun. Decide in advance what happens when a storm is forecast: many owners take the dish down and store it, which is only practical if the mount and cable were planned for that.

The router is not a home network

The router supplied with Starlink covers a small area. In a concrete-block house it will not reach the bedrooms, let alone the terrace or guest cottage. The answer is the same as with any provider: put the Starlink equipment into bypass mode and connect it to a proper router, with wired access points placed for coverage. Depending on the dish model you may need Starlink’s Ethernet adapter to do this.

If the Wi-Fi in a Starlink home is poor, the satellite link is rarely the problem. Our article on why Wi-Fi struggles in concrete homes explains what is.

Cameras and remote access

Standard Starlink service does not give your home a public address you can connect to directly, so older camera recorders and remote-access methods that rely on port forwarding will not work. Modern systems connect outward to a cloud service instead and are unaffected. If you are moving an existing camera or automation system to Starlink, have this checked before the old connection is cancelled.

Power and weather

Starlink needs constant power for the dish and router. Put both on a UPS along with the network equipment, so a brief outage does not take the internet down for several minutes while everything restarts. See backup power for smart homes.

Very heavy rain can slow or interrupt the connection for a short time. For most homes that is acceptable. For a home office or a property with monitored security, it is a reason to keep a second connection.

Two connections are better than one

A router that supports automatic failover can use Starlink alongside fibre, cable or a cellular connection and switch between them without anyone noticing. In Nassau, Starlink makes an excellent backup to a wired provider. In the Family Islands, a cellular connection can back up Starlink. Either way the house stays online when one service has a bad day.

Family Island homes

For homes in Exuma, Eleuthera, Abaco and the other islands, Starlink is often the connection that makes remote management possible at all: checking cameras, adjusting climate to control humidity, and letting us support the system from Nassau. We design these installations to be self-reliant, with spares on site and clear instructions for a caretaker. Read more about our work in the Family Islands.

Common questions

Can I install Starlink myself?

Yes, and many people do. Where a professional adds value is the permanent mount, the cable route, the whole-home network behind it and the failover setup.

Will my smart home work on Starlink?

Yes. Control systems, streaming, cameras and smart locks work normally. Only older remote-access methods need changing.

Should I replace my existing provider with Starlink?

If you have reliable fibre, keep it as the main connection and consider Starlink as the backup. Where wired service is slow or unavailable, Starlink is usually the better main connection.
