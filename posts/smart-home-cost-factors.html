What Drives the Cost of a Smart Home in the Bahamas?

Every property is different, so an honest answer is a list of what moves the number rather than a single figure. These are the decisions that matter, and how to phase a project sensibly.

By Island IntegrationNov 16, 20263 min read

“How much does a smart home cost?” is the first question most people ask, and the hardest to answer in one line. A single media room with a good remote and a whole estate with lighting, shades, audio in every room and cameras are both “a smart home”. What we can do is show you what drives the budget, so you can decide where your money does the most good.

1. How many rooms and which systems

Scope is the biggest factor by far. Each room you add to audio, video or lighting control adds equipment, cabling and programming. Each subsystem you include, such as lighting, shades, climate, audio, cameras and access, is a project in its own right. Start by listing what you want each room to do rather than which products you want.

2. New build or existing home

In a new build, cable goes in before the walls close and costs comparatively little. In a finished concrete-block home, every new cable run means chasing walls or finding another route, so retrofits lean on wireless lighting, battery shades and careful planning. Neither is wrong, but the labour looks very different. If you are building, our pre-wire checklist is the cheapest insurance you can buy.

3. Lighting control

Lighting is often the largest single line in a whole-home project, because it touches every room. Costs follow the number of circuits being controlled, whether the system is centralised or wireless, and how many keypads there are. It is also the system people say they would not give up afterwards.

4. The network

A professional network with wired access points, managed switching and a proper rack is not optional in a connected home, and it is where a tight budget is most tempting to cut. Do not. Almost every complaint about an unreliable smart home traces back to the network. See estate Wi-Fi and networking.

5. Equipment level

Speakers, displays, projectors and amplifiers all come in good, better and best. In most rooms good is genuinely good. Spend at the higher level where you will notice it: the main living area, a dedicated cinema, outdoor audio that has to survive salt air.

What is different in the Bahamas

  • Freight and duty. Equipment is imported, and shipping and customs charges are part of every quotation.
  • Coastal-grade equipment. Outdoor speakers, cameras and access points need to be rated for salt air, and those versions cost more. See protecting electronics from salt air.
  • Power protection. Surge protection and UPS for racks and network equipment are essentials here, not extras.
  • Family Island projects. Travel, accommodation and freight to the island are real costs that careful planning keeps down.

Where to spend first

If the budget will not cover everything at once, phase it. First the infrastructure that is hard to add later: cabling, conduit, the equipment rack and the network. Then the systems you will use every day, usually the main living area and lighting in key rooms. Leave rooms that can be added without opening walls for later. A system designed in full and installed in stages costs less overall than one that grows by accident.

Ongoing costs

Plan for a modest amount of upkeep: software updates, the occasional battery or remote, changes when a television or cable box is replaced, and optional remote monitoring. Some manufacturer apps charge a subscription for remote access. We set all of this out in the proposal so there are no surprises.

Common questions

Can you give me a rough figure without a visit?

We can give a sensible range once we know the size of the property, the rooms involved and whether it is a new build. A firm figure needs plans or a site visit.

Is it cheaper to buy the equipment myself?

Sometimes the sticker price is lower, but warranty, compatibility and freight problems then sit with you. See professional versus DIY.

Want a figure for your property?

Tell us about the house and what you want it to do, and we will prepare a clear, itemised proposal.
