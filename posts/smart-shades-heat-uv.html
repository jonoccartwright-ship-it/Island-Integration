Motorized Shades: Beat the Heat, Glare and UV

Sunshine is a great thing about the Bahamas, but it is hard on furniture, floors and your cooling bill. Motorized shades give you control over it.

By Island IntegrationNov 2, 20265 min read

Why automate shades?

  • Heat: blocking direct sun reduces the load on your air conditioning.
  • Glare: comfortable screens and TVs without squinting.
  • UV: helps slow fading of art, wood floors and fabric.
  • Privacy: close all the shades in an area in one action.

The honest answer to “why motorize?” is that manual shades do not get used. A wall of glass facing the sea might have six or eight shades across it. Nobody adjusts each one twice a day, so they stay up and the sun pours in, or they stay down and the view is lost. Motorized shades move at the right time without anyone thinking about it.

In the Bahamas the sun is strong year-round, and west- and south-facing rooms take the brunt of it in the afternoon, exactly when air conditioning is working hardest. Lowering a solar shade before the sun reaches the glass stops much of that heat at the window instead of asking the air conditioner to remove it afterward.

Choosing fabrics

Fabric choice is a trade-off. Solar or screen fabrics keep a view while cutting glare and heat, blackout fabrics suit bedrooms and cinema rooms, and many homes use both: screen shades by day and blackout for night.

Screen fabrics are described by their openness factor, the percentage of the weave that is open. A tighter weave blocks more light and heat but softens the view; a more open weave keeps the view sharp but lets more glare through. Colour matters too: darker screens give a clearer view out and better glare control, while lighter screens reflect more heat but can look hazy in bright sun. For a living room looking over the water, a dark, fairly tight screen is often the right balance. For a bedroom you want full blackout, and a dual shade, with a screen and a blackout fabric on one bracket, lets you have both.

Think about the facade as well: a consistent fabric colour across the windows looks far better from the pool deck than a mix.

Power options

Shades can be wired or battery-powered. Wired shades avoid battery changes but need power planned at the window, so decide early during construction, as covered in the pre-wire checklist. Battery shades suit retrofits.

For a new build or major renovation, wired is the right choice. Low-voltage wiring to each window pocket is inexpensive while the walls are open and hard to add neatly afterward in a concrete-block house. Wired motors are quiet, need no battery checks, and can be fed from a backed-up circuit so the shades still work during a BPL outage.

Battery motors have improved a lot and suit an existing home where chasing wire through block walls is not practical. Expect to recharge them a few times a year. In a second home that is empty for months, that is one more thing for the caretaker to remember, so we steer owners toward wired where there is any way to do it. Our system design and structured cabling service plans the power and control wiring alongside everything else before the slab is poured.

Schedules and scenes

Shades can follow the sun’s position through the day, and respond to scenes such as “Movie” or “Away”. Combined with smart climate control, this makes the house more comfortable and efficient with no daily effort.

A typical setup: east-facing shades lower in the early morning and rise once the sun has moved on. South and west shades lower in the early afternoon and rise toward sunset so you get the view at golden hour. Bedroom blackouts close at bedtime and open with the morning alarm. The schedule shifts with the season automatically because it is tied to sunrise and sunset, not the clock.

Scenes tie shades to the rest of the house. “Movie” closes the cinema blackout and dims the lights. “Away” closes everything to protect furniture and keep the house cooler while you are gone. “Good night” closes the bedroom shades and locks the doors. When shades are part of a Control4 smart home automation system, all of this runs from one app, one keypad or one voice command, and the motorized shades become one piece of a house that manages itself.

What to look for

  • Quiet motors. You should not hear a shade moving from the next room, especially in bedrooms.
  • Proper measurement and brackets. A shade that is a few millimetres off rubs on the pocket or leaves a light gap. Measuring and installation are where most problems start.
  • Corrosion-resistant hardware. Brackets, chains and fasteners near open windows or on a covered terrace need to handle salt air.
  • Integration. Shades should speak to the same system as your lighting and climate, not live in a separate app.
  • Local support. A motor that fails under warranty is only useful if someone in Nassau can replace it.

Common mistakes

The most common one is leaving shades to the end of the project, when the window pockets are finished and there is no power at the head of the window, so the only option is battery. The second is choosing fabric from a small swatch indoors; a screen that looks fine in the showroom can look very different against a bright Bahamian sky, so we bring larger samples to the window. The third is treating every window the same. A pool-facing wall, a bedroom and a home office each have different needs.

A note on storms

Interior shades are not a substitute for hurricane shutters or impact-rated protection. Follow your builder’s and local authorities’ guidance for storm protection.

What shades can do during hurricane season is add a layer of protection for the interior: closed blackout shades reduce the chance of glass fragments spreading if a window does fail, and they keep the sun off furniture while the house is shut up. Our hurricane season guide covers how we prepare a smart home before a storm.

Frequently asked questions

Can I add motorized shades to an existing home?

Yes. Battery-powered shades are the usual answer for retrofits. Where there is an accessible ceiling or an existing wire path, we can sometimes hard-wire them.

Do the shades work if the internet is down?

Schedules and keypads run locally, so the shades keep moving. You only lose remote control from your phone while the connection is out.

Can shades help with cooling costs?

They reduce the heat entering the house through the glass, which is one of the biggest loads on air conditioning here. Paired with smart climate control, that is a real saving over a summer.
